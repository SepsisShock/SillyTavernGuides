Sampler Settings

Ever wondered what Temperature, Top K, or those penalty sliders actually change? This guide walks you through them, and you can play with the sliders to see the difference.

What is a sampler?

When the AI replies, it builds its message a little at a time. Each time, it looks at several possible pieces of text and picks one. These pieces are called tokens. A token can be a word, part of a word, or punctuation. Each possible token has a different chance of being picked.

Sampler settings change how the next token is chosen. Think of the AI as choosing from a menu. Temperature changes how tempting each option looks, Top K and Top P shorten the menu, and penalties make repeated choices less tempting.

Temperature

Changes how strongly the AI favors its most likely choices.

Top K & Top P

Narrow down which choices are allowed.

Frequency & Presence

Discourage repeating tokens the AI has already encountered.

None of these settings makes the AI smarter or teaches it new information. They just affect how it picks what comes next.

What should you notice?

  • Temperature: Changing it rearranges the odds, even if the same words remain available.
  • Top K: Setting it to 2 allows no more than the two most likely candidates.
  • Top P: Lowering it removes less likely options until only enough candidates remain to meet the probability threshold.
  • Frequency Penalty: “cookies” loses more ground than “cake” because it appeared more often.
  • Presence Penalty: “cookies,” “bread,” and “cake” are all discouraged for having appeared, regardless of how many times.

Why do some remaining bars grow after filtering? When some words are removed, the remaining chances have to add up to 100% again. That makes their bars longer, but it doesn't mean the AI suddenly understands the story better.

Temperature: How predictable are the choices?

Imagine the AI strongly favors “cookies,” but “pie” and “humans” are also on the list. With a low Temperature, “cookies” is even more likely to win. Raise it, and the less obvious choices get more of a chance.

Lower than 1: More predictable choices. 1: Keeps the original relative probabilities. Higher than 1: Flattens the probabilities, making unusual choices more competitive.

Turning Temperature up doesn't make a character smarter or more creative by itself. It may make the reply more surprising, but it can also make it weirder in ways you didn't want.

Top K and Top P: Who gets to stay?

Both settings narrow the list of possible next choices. The difference is how they decide what stays.

Top K counts candidates

If Top K is 3, only the three highest-ranked candidates can be selected. Top K cares about the number of choices, not how far apart their chances are.

Top P adds probabilities

If Top P is 0.80, the sampler works down the ranked list until the accumulated probability reaches at least 80%. Sometimes that takes only two choices. Other times, it takes many more.

If you use both, they can narrow the choices together. Different backends may apply them in different orders.

Neither slider knows whether a word is good for your scene. They simply decide which possibilities are still available.

Frequency and Presence: What has the AI said already?

Both try to discourage repetition, but they pay attention to different things.

Frequency Penalty asks, “How many times?”

Every repeat matters. Since “cookies” has appeared three times in our example and “cake” only once, “cookies” gets a bigger penalty.

Presence Penalty asks, “Has it appeared at all?”

This one only checks whether the word has shown up before. Once is enough. Five appearances don't make the presence penalty five times stronger.

Be careful with strong penalties in roleplay. Characters need to use names and return to important details. Trying too hard to prevent repeats can make dialogue awkward.

What about Repetition Penalty? It also discourages reused tokens, but it works differently from Frequency and Presence Penalties. You may see it on text-generation backends; don't assume the three settings are interchangeable.

The other samplers, briefly

You might spot some of these in SillyTavern too. You don't need to learn them all right now. This is just a quick reference for when a name looks unfamiliar.

SettingWhat it does
Min PDrops choices that are much less likely than the best one.
Typical PTries to keep choices that are neither too obvious nor too surprising.
Top AUses the best choice's chance to decide which weaker choices to remove.
Tail Free SamplingTries to cut off the long list of increasingly unlikely choices.
Dynamic TemperatureLets Temperature change automatically within the limits you set.

Do you need to change these settings?

Maybe not! Some models work best with the settings recommended by their creators, and not every API lets you change every sampler. There isn't one magic preset that works for all models.

Start with what your model supports

First, check whether your model or API provider recommends particular values. If it does, start there. If a setting is missing or unsupported, you don't need to force it.

Want to experiment? Keep the prompt the same and change one setting at a time. That way, you can actually tell what changed. And remember, a different reply isn't necessarily a better one.

Keep in mind: The playground shows the general idea, not a perfect copy of every backend. Which sliders work, and in what order, depends on how you connect SillyTavern to your model.

The three things to remember

1. Variety

Temperature changes how competitive unusual choices are.

2. Filtering

Top K and Top P decide which candidates are still in the running.

3. Repetition

Frequency, Presence, and other penalties discourage reuse in different ways.

You don't need to fiddle with every slider to get a good reply. Start with your model's recommended settings, then experiment if you have a reason to.
